# Testing: strategy, levels and test data

Objective served: a guide that supports Bernardo and the agents building the app when they decide what to test, at which level, and with what data. Mode: broad. Evaluating AI output belongs to Applied AI, and CI pipelines to Delivery.

Findings are marked **Source**, **Synthesis** (a pattern across sources) or **Inference** (a consequence drawn from the evidence). Citations give the source, the section and, where the collection recorded them, the lines of the collected copy, with the link to the original.

## 1. The answer

The evidence is testing-strategy essays (Fowler, Google, Vocke, Dodds), tool and framework documentation, a few papers and individual issue reports. It describes when each practice pays off and what it cannot show; it does not establish one best shape for every app. Where the table below speaks of "pays off" it is the sources' conditions, not a measured result.

### 1.1 Decisions to settle first

A synthesis across the views below, not any source's own list.

1. **What each level exercises in this app.** Terms such as unit, integration, system and end-to-end differ across sources; agree locally what each level covers before assigning tests to it. (1.2)
2. **For the rules whose wrong output is costly (prices, discounts, validity), which methods and which product contract.** Examples, properties and mutation checks need expected results (rounding policy, whether start and end dates are inclusive) that the sources cannot supply. (1.3)
3. **How time is controlled and where the real scheduler is tested.** Fake clocks and workflow-engine time skipping test rules, not a deployed scheduler. (1.3)
4. **How messages are kept from real people.** An explicitly non-delivering backend or simulator, with delivery behavior checked separately from message content. (1.3)
5. **How run-to-run variation is controlled.** Inject time and randomness, isolate state, assert order only if order is the contract, and diagnose intermittent failures before adding retries. (1.3)
6. **Which data approach for which purpose.** Explicit fixtures and generated inputs by default; realistic fake values for presentation; copied, pseudonymised or synthetic data only where its fidelity justifies a reviewed privacy assessment. (1.4)

### 1.2 Levels and strategies: where each pays off

**Synthesis:** Build a risk-focused portfolio, not a fixed pyramid or trophy ratio. Keep frequent checks at the lowest level that gives the needed confidence; add integration checks where boundaries, persistence, or external behavior matter; use browser/UI checks for user-visible behavior and reserve E2E checks for a small number of critical user journeys. A broader test pays off when it covers a failure mode the lower level cannot observe. Broad tests cost more to set up and maintain and can fail because of timing, environment, or shared state. Fowler allows a reliable, inexpensive high-level suite to change the balance; the sources do not establish one best shape for every app. ([Fowler, “Test Pyramid,” assumptions and exceptions](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/TestPyramid.html), lines 78–84, 91, 106; [Fowler, “On the Diverse and Fantastical Shapes of Testing,” test shapes and boundaries](https://martinfowler.com/articles/2021-test-shapes.html), lines 81–107.)

The terms *unit*, *integration*, *system*, and *end-to-end* have differing scopes across sources. Agree on what each level exercises in this app; test size (how much runs) and scope (which parts interact) are related but not identical. In this guide, a system/API check exercises assembled application behavior at a service boundary; a browser/UI check exercises rendered, user-visible behavior; an end-to-end (E2E) check exercises a complete critical journey across features. These are useful distinctions, not universal definitions: UI and E2E are orthogonal, so a UI check need not cover a whole journey and an E2E check need not use a browser. (Synthesis of [Fowler, “Test Pyramid,” assumptions and exceptions](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/TestPyramid.html), lines 78–84; [Vocke, “The Practical Test Pyramid,” levels and terminology](https://martinfowler.com/articles/practical-test-pyramid.html), lines 671–702, 862–882; [Django testing tools](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/6.0/topics/testing/tools/), “The test client,” lines 53–71; [React Testing Library introduction](https://testing-library.com/docs/react-testing-library/intro/), “This solution,” lines 77–90.)

The comparison below is a **synthesis** of these sources; the final column gives supporting sources and their limits.

| Level or strategy | Use it when it pays off | Main risk it covers | Limits and source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unit/component** | A rule or component can be exercised with controlled inputs and a clear expected result. Use for frequent examples, boundaries, and regressions. | Incorrect decisions inside a component, such as a price calculation or eligibility cutoff. | It cannot establish that collaborators, persistence, or the whole journey work together. [ISTQB glossary](https://api.glossary.istqb.org/storage/help/R0uz58NqLzUz48LVUuyGSF76NFj4LHQazSs0GlNS.pdf), PDF pp. 5, 11; [Rails testing guide](https://guides.rubyonrails.org/testing.html), sections 6, 17.2. |
| **Integration/boundary** | A risk lies in interactions: application-to-database, serialization, queue/provider adapter, or a small group of components. Keep dependable, fast dependencies real when fidelity matters; replace slow, flaky, or side-effecting dependencies when testing application decisions. | Mismatched assumptions at boundaries, persistence/serialization errors, wrong wiring, or failure handling. | A double only verifies the behavior encoded in the double. A real dependency raises setup cost and still does not prove full user behavior. [Vocke](https://martinfowler.com/articles/practical-test-pyramid.html), lines 375–409; [Vitest, “When to Mock”](https://main.vitest.dev/guide/learn/testing-in-practice), lines 313–332; [Software Engineering at Google, ch. 14](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch14.html), lines 25–37, 123–129. |
| **System/API check** | Use when behavior depends on the integrated application at its external service/API boundary, but a browser or complete user journey adds no needed confidence. | Cross-component behavior or service-contract failures in the application as a system. | “System” is not used consistently as a distinct tier, so define the exercised boundary locally. This check does not establish rendered UI behavior or a full user journey. An API/service layer can retain some E2E benefits without UI-framework complexity. [Vocke](https://martinfowler.com/articles/practical-test-pyramid.html), lines 671–702, 862–882; [Fowler, “Test Pyramid”](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/TestPyramid.html), lines 78–84. |
| **Browser/UI behavior** | Use when rendered content, controls, JavaScript behavior, or a user-visible result matters. A component, page, or browser check can cover this without traversing a cross-feature journey. | Incorrect or broken user-visible behavior, including rendering and browser-side interactions. | It does not by itself establish server-side persistence or a complete journey across features. Browser/system checks are slower and can be brittle around timing and UI changes; isolate their state. [Django testing tools](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/6.0/topics/testing/tools/), “The test client,” lines 53–71; [React Testing Library](https://testing-library.com/docs/react-testing-library/intro/), “This solution,” lines 77–90; [Playwright best practices](https://playwright.dev/docs/best-practices), “Testing philosophy” and “Make tests as isolated as possible,” lines 84–90; [Rails testing guide](https://guides.rubyonrails.org/testing.html), sections 7 and 7.1. |
| **End-to-end (E2E) journey** | Use a small set of critical journeys that combine features and tasks across the whole system. The journey can run through a browser or a service/API boundary, depending on what the risk requires. | Broken cross-feature workflow and failures in how the whole system behaves along a critical user path. | Broader tests cost more to set up and maintain and can be brittle from timing, browser quirks, or shared state. Prefer a lower level when it gives the needed confidence; reproduce high-level defects lower when possible. [Google, “How Much Testing Is Enough?”](https://testing.googleblog.com/2021/06/how-much-testing-is-enough.html), lines 26–34; [Vocke](https://martinfowler.com/articles/practical-test-pyramid.html), lines 693–702; [Fowler, “Test Pyramid”](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/TestPyramid.html), lines 78–84. |
| **Property-based testing** | A rule should hold across many valid inputs or action sequences, and hand-picked examples are too sparse. Generate values or stateful sequences, assert the property, and retain/shrink failing cases for replay. | Unanticipated combinations and edge values; a faulty invariant across a range of inputs. | Sampling is finite, not exhaustive. The property/model can itself be wrong or too similar to the system. Keep explicit examples for named business boundaries. [fast-check, “What is property-based testing?”](https://fast-check.dev/docs/introduction/what-is-property-based/), “Examples versus properties,” “sample coverage and shrinking”; [Hypothesis, stateful testing](https://hypothesis.readthedocs.io/en/latest/stateful.html), “Stateful tests”; [fast-check, model-based testing](https://fast-check.dev/docs/advanced/model-based-testing/), overview and model cautions. |
| **Mutation testing** | A high-cost rule has tests, but it is unclear whether their assertions would catch plausible mistakes. Check whether selected code changes (“mutants”) cause the tests to fail. | Weak or missing assertions around consequential logic. | Killing selected mutants is evidence about those mutations only; a score does not prove correctness. [mutmut documentation](https://mutmut.readthedocs.io/en/latest/), overview and workflow. |

These strategies can combine: a unit-level property test can explore a calculation; an integration test can check its persistence; a browser test can verify the customer sees the resulting total. **Inference:** choose each layer for its distinct observation, not to duplicate the same assertion at every level. This follows Fowler’s “lower when sufficient confidence is possible” rule and Google’s distinction between cohesive integration checks and critical end-to-end journeys. ([Vocke](https://martinfowler.com/articles/practical-test-pyramid.html), lines 375–409; [Google, “How Much Testing Is Enough?”](https://testing.googleblog.com/2021/06/how-much-testing-is-enough.html), lines 26–34.)

### 1.3 What to test for the stated priorities

#### Prices, discounts, and validity rules

- **Source-backed methods:** Use focused unit examples with explicit inputs and expected outcomes; add properties for rules that should hold across a range; use mutation checks when the consequence of a missed assertion is high. Property testing complements examples and shrinks a failing generated case; it does not replace chosen boundary cases. ([fast-check, “What is property-based testing?”](https://fast-check.dev/docs/introduction/what-is-property-based-testing/), “Examples versus properties,” “sample coverage and shrinking”; [Hypothesis, introduction](https://hypothesis.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorial/introduction.html), “When to use Hypothesis and property-based testing”; [mutmut](https://mutmut.readthedocs.io/en/latest/), workflow.)
- **Specific cases (inference from the documented mechanisms and collected incidents):** Encode the product’s rounding policy, then cover half-unit rounding, currency precision, inclusive/exclusive validity start and end, and combinations such as discounts plus tax, multiple lines, and high quantities. Check both the stated expected total and properties such as “a discount does not increase the eligible total” where that is the intended rule. Decimal documentation supplies explicit quantization/rounding controls, while issue reports show that quantity-amplified precision and multi-line global rounding can change totals. ([Python `decimal`](https://docs.python.org/3.11/library/decimal.html), `quantize()` monetary example and contexts/traps; [Odoo issue #288960](https://github.com/odoo/odoo/issues/288960), reproduction and regression outputs, lines 305–310, 338–352, 375–397; [Odoo issue #266745](https://github.com/odoo/odoo/issues/266745), multi-line example, lines 191–250.)
- **At integration level:** Check persisted and reloaded values, database constraints, and date/money serialization against the database type used by the app when those boundaries could alter the result. A disposable real database is one documented way to test those interactions. ([Testcontainers JDBC support](https://java.testcontainers.org/modules/databases/jdbc/), lines 195–203, 291–296; [Vocke](https://martinfowler.com/articles/practical-test-pyramid.html), lines 375–409.)
- **At the system/API boundary:** Check the calculation and resulting persisted or returned amount across the assembled application when service wiring or contracts are at risk; this does not require a browser or a complete journey.
- **At browser/UI and E2E levels:** Check that the customer sees the correct total in a browser/UI test. Add a small number of complete purchase or renewal journeys to verify the displayed and final charged/recorded result across features. **Inference** based on Fowler’s distinction between UI behavior and E2E scope and Google’s recommendation to reserve E2E checks for critical journeys. ([Fowler, “Test Pyramid”](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/TestPyramid.html), lines 78–84; [Google, “How Much Testing Is Enough?”](https://testing.googleblog.com/2021/06/how-much-testing-is-enough.html), lines 32–34.)

The sources do not set the correct currency, tax, discount, or date-validity policy for this product. Those expectations must come from the product rules; the Odoo incidents are counterexamples to simplistic coverage, not universal expected results.

#### Reminders, expiries, and flows across time

- **Unit/component:** Inject a clock or fake timer. Check immediately before, at, and after the deadline; assert eligibility, expiry, and reminder behavior without waiting in real time. For local-calendar rules, include month boundaries and timezone/DST transitions. Clock injection, fake timers, and travel-to-time techniques are documented by [Oracle `Clock`](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/17/docs/api/java.base/java/time/Clock.html), `fixed` and `offset`; [Microsoft `FakeTimeProvider`](https://learn.microsoft.com/dotnet/core/extensions/timeprovider-testing?tabs=dotnet-cli), lines 32–45; [Jest timer mocks](https://jestjs.io/docs/timer-mocks), “Advance Timers by Time” and “Run Pending Timers”; and [Rails](https://guides.rubyonrails.org/testing.html), section 17.2.
- **Stateful behavior:** If the sequence matters, generate or specify transitions such as create → extend → expire → renew and check that invalid transitions do not occur. Use a state machine only when a simpler property cannot express the behavior. ([Hypothesis stateful testing](https://hypothesis.readthedocs.io/en/latest/stateful.html), “Stateful tests” and “You may not need stateful tests”; [fast-check model-based testing](https://fast-check.dev/docs/advanced/model-based-testing/), command definitions and model cautions.)
- **Integration and system/API boundary:** Exercise the actual scheduling/workflow boundary and its persisted state when reminders or expiries depend on a queue, scheduler, or workflow engine. For durable workflows, engine test environments can fast-forward timers and inspect intermediate states, but this does not establish deployed scheduler behavior. ([Temporal TypeScript testing suite](https://github.com/temporalio/documentation/blob/main/docs/develop/typescript/best-practices/testing-suite.mdx), automatic/manual skipping and intermediate state; [Temporal Java testing suite](https://github.com/temporalio/documentation/blob/main/docs/develop/java/best-practices/testing-suite.mdx), “How to skip time,” global clock and `Workflow.sleep()` limits.)
- **Browser/UI or E2E when user experience is at risk:** Check the displayed expiry/reminder state in a browser/UI test; use an E2E journey when the risk is that a scheduled transition fails across the full workflow. Simulated time can speed up the clock-dependent part, but does not establish deployed scheduler behavior. **Inference** from Rails’ user-driven system-test examples and Google’s critical-journey guidance. ([Rails testing guide](https://guides.rubyonrails.org/testing.html), sections 7 and 17.2; [Google, “How Much Testing Is Enough?”](https://testing.googleblog.com/2021/06/how-much-testing-is-enough.html), lines 32–34.)
- **Time-zone semantics:** Specify whether a reminder follows elapsed duration or local wall-clock time; test the chosen interpretation around DST gaps/overlaps. `zoneinfo` documents repeated local times and `fold`, while CPython issue reports illustrate surprising arithmetic and construction of nonexistent local times. ([Python `zoneinfo`](https://docs.python.org/3.14/library/zoneinfo.html), arithmetic/DST and `fold`; [CPython #91618](https://github.com/python/cpython/issues/91618), reproduction and maintainer response; [CPython #116038](https://github.com/python/cpython/issues/116038), London DST-gap examples.)

**Limit:** Fake clocks and timer APIs do not by themselves test a separate scheduler or prove real elapsed-time behavior in production. Temporal forum and issue reports describe cases where SDK/test-harness setup, workflow shape, signals, or mocked activities affected time-skipping tests. They are individual reports, not evidence that virtual time is generally unreliable. ([Temporal Ruby #360](https://github.com/temporalio/sdk-ruby/issues/360), report and context; [Temporal forum, child workflow](https://community.temporal.io/t/how-can-i-start-and-abandon-a-child-workflow-but-still-make-it-a-skip-time-in-unit-tests/11007), original report; [Temporal PHP #743](https://github.com/temporalio/sdk-php/issues/743), report and workaround.)

#### Messages that must not reach real people

- **Unit/component:** Assert message recipient, subject, body, headers, attachments, and dispatch decision against an in-memory outbox or a stubbed sender. Django’s in-memory backend stores messages for inspection; pytest demonstrates replacing a network call. These tests cover construction and application decisions, not external delivery. ([Django email documentation](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/dev/topics/email/), “In-memory backend,” lines 460–469; [pytest monkeypatch](https://docs.pytest.org/en/stable/how-to/monkeypatch.html), network mock and explicit-dependency guidance, lines 212–232.)
- **Integration:** Route messages to a local SMTP sink and assert on the captured content and error handling; Mailpit documents API inspection and simulated SMTP failures. For provider-specific callback behavior, a provider simulator such as SES can exercise success, bounce, automatic response, and complaint outcomes without real recipients. ([Mailpit integration testing](https://mailpit.axllent.org/docs/integration/), lines 4–26; [Mailpit local SMTP sink](https://mailpit.axllent.org/docs/install/testing/), lines 3–24; [Amazon SES mailbox simulator](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/send-an-email-from-console.html), lines 55–80.)
- **Boundary:** Keep checks of provider delivery behavior distinct from checks of the app’s message content. A local sink cannot establish deliverability; SES simulator behavior is provider-specific and still has account rate/billing constraints. Do not treat either as a check of inbox placement. **Inference:** to make “without sending” dependable, tests need an explicitly non-delivering backend or simulator configuration; the sources document these mechanisms but do not certify this app’s configuration. (Same Mailpit and SES sources above.)
- **Webhooks, if the app handles them:** Test parsing/decision logic with fixed provider payloads, then test the local handler’s signature, duplicates, delay, and out-of-order behavior against the provider contract. GitHub documents these delivery conditions, but its test workflow can create real objects in a test repository and other providers differ. ([GitHub testing webhooks](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/testing-and-troubleshooting-webhooks/testing-webhooks), “About testing webhooks” and local proxy workflow; [GitHub troubleshooting webhooks](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/testing-and-troubleshooting-webhooks/troubleshooting-webhooks), timeout, delayed/out-of-order delivery, signatures.)

#### Results that can vary between runs

First control variation that is not the behavior under test: inject time, seed or record randomness, isolate state and dependencies, and make test data independent. pytest issue #667 shows a practitioner case where execution order changed random values and obscured failures; Vitest recommends controlling date, random, and UUID inputs; Playwright recommends isolated data, cookies, and storage. ([pytest #667](https://github.com/pytest-dev/pytest/issues/667), report and seed practice, lines 185–245; [Vitest](https://main.vitest.dev/guide/learn/testing-in-practice), “Non-Deterministic Values,” lines 322–324; [Playwright best practices](https://playwright.dev/docs/best-practices), “Make tests as isolated as possible,” lines 84–90.)

Where output is genuinely variable, assert stable requirements or relationships instead of one exact output. Examples supported in the sources include goal-based postconditions, metamorphic relations across runs, and statistical checks for stochastic output. Such checks need a justified oracle and, for statistical tests, enough trials for the target confidence; no generic threshold is supplied. ([SEI, “The Challenges of Testing in a Non-Deterministic World”](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/blog/the-challenges-of-testing-in-a-non-deterministic-world/), lines 75–83, 97–111; [SEI, “Seven Recommendations…”](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/blog/seven-recommendations-for-testing-in-a-non-deterministic-world/), lines 74–92; [Patel and Hierons](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11219-019-09459-0), lines 41–42, 67–81.)

**Synthesis:** For order-sensitive operations, assert ordering only when order is part of the product contract; otherwise assert the required set/state or invariant. Isolate shared resources and include delayed/duplicate/out-of-order external events where the provider documents them. The sources establish those risks in particular test and webhook contexts, not their frequency in this app. Retries/quarantine can reduce false alarms but can also hide races or defects; a Cypress race report and an older Hypothesis timing report illustrate why an intermittent failure should be diagnosed rather than automatically dismissed. ([Google, “Flaky Tests at Google…”](https://www.googblogs.com/flaky-tests-at-google-and-how-we-mitigate-them/), lines 17–23; [Cypress #25470](https://github.com/cypress-io/cypress/issues/25470), lines 161–188; [Hypothesis #2108](https://github.com/HypothesisWorks/hypothesis/issues/2108), lines 160–209.)

AI-output quality is owned by Applied AI and is outside this digest. The variable-output guidance here concerns time, random values, ordering, concurrency, and external behavior.

### 1.4 Test-data decisions

| Data approach | When it pays off | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Hand-built fixtures/factories** | Build small, domain-shaped records with explicit consequential values and relationships; override price, discount, validity, status, or locale for the case. Factories can keep setup focused and reproducible. | A factory does not establish adequate coverage; random defaults can hide the case under test. [Factory Boy](https://factoryboy.readthedocs.io/en/stable/index.html), introduction and [reference](https://factoryboy.readthedocs.io/en/stable/reference.html), traits/randomness management. |
| **Fake realistic values** | Populate UI, names, addresses, and ordinary scenarios when plausibility helps. Seed and pin versions when replay matters; handle uniqueness explicitly. | Faker-style data may collide, may change across versions, and does not ensure edge coverage or safe destinations. [Faker Python](https://faker.readthedocs.io/en/stable/index.html), seeding/uniqueness; [Faker.js](https://fakerjs.dev/guide/unique.html), “Unique Values”; [fast-check fake data](https://fast-check.dev/docs/advanced/fake-data/), lines 24–63. |
| **Property generators** | Explore valid boundaries and combinations for rules; seed/replay and shrink a failing case. Constrain generators to the product’s valid domain. | Generated values do not replace carefully selected examples; a property only helps if the expected rule is correct. [fast-check](https://fast-check.dev/docs/introduction/what-is-property-based/), sample coverage/shrinking; [Hypothesis strategies](https://hypothesis.readthedocs.io/en/latest/reference/strategies.html), dates/datetimes. |
| **Disposable real services** | Use a temporary instance of the real database where query, constraint, serialization, or persistence fidelity matters. | More setup/runtime; does not exercise an external mail provider or full user flow. [Testcontainers JDBC](https://java.testcontainers.org/modules/databases/jdbc/), lines 195–203, 291–296; [Software Engineering at Google, ch. 14](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch14.html), fidelity discussion, lines 25–37. |
| **Copied or pseudonymised records** | Consider only when real distributions or production-shaped complexity are necessary and a reviewed risk assessment supports that use. Minimise personal information in development. | Removing direct identifiers does not necessarily anonymise records; contextual re-identification risk remains. [ICO, privacy in product design](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/designing-products-that-protect-privacy/privacy-in-the-product-design-lifecycle/), lines 23–30; [ICO, effective anonymisation](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/how-do-we-ensure-anonymisation-is-effective/), lines 237–289, 307–338; [ICO, pseudonymisation](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/pseudonymisation), lines 235–270. |
| **Synthetic datasets** | May provide useful population-shaped data without directly copying records, if the generation method and utility/privacy requirements fit. | “Synthetic” does not certify anonymity: models trained on real records can carry disclosure risk, and utility/privacy trade-offs are method-dependent. Available research includes an abstract-only study and one full-text critical analysis, not a general review. ([Stadler et al., “Synthetic Data — Anonymisation Groundhog Day”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.07018), abstract; [Ricciato, “Privacy-Enhancing or Privacy-Elusion Technology?”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0282423X251412328), utility/privacy distinction and conclusion, lines 37–66, 90–91.) |

**Synthesis:** Prefer explicit fixtures and generated inputs for ordinary test cases; use realistic fake values for presentation; add reviewed production-shaped or synthetic data only where its extra fidelity justifies the privacy assessment. Test datasets should be isolated and repeatable. Do not send generated-looking email addresses to real delivery paths: realistic-looking data is not a non-delivery guarantee. The latter is an inference from the documented Faker collision limits and the separate local-sink/simulator mechanisms.

### 1.5 Where sources diverge

- **Portfolio shape:** The pyramid favors a broad base and cautions against slow, brittle GUI checks; the trophy favors relatively more integration tests in a frontend/UI context. Fowler’s later essay and Vocke point out that teams use different definitions and test boundaries. Treat these as context-specific heuristics rather than competing universal prescriptions. ([Fowler, “Test Pyramid,” lines 78–106](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/TestPyramid.html); [Dodds, “Testing Trophy,” lines 37–49, 70–107](https://kentcdodds.com/blog/the-testing-trophy-and-testing-classifications); [Fowler, “Diverse Shapes,” lines 81–107](https://martinfowler.com/articles/2021-test-shapes.html).)
- **TDD:** Fowler and Beck defend test-first/self-testing practices in a practitioner discussion; Hansson raises concerns about mock-heavy design, indirection, and maintenance. The source is a debate, not empirical resolution. Keep the distinction between the value of tests and requiring test-first development. ([“Is TDD Dead?”](https://martinfowler.com/articles/is-tdd-dead/), “Where This Came From,” “Test-induced design damage,” and “Costs of Testing.”)
- **Test doubles and fidelity:** Fake dependencies improve speed/control and enable difficult scenarios, but a double can diverge from the real dependency. Use a double for application decisions and a real boundary/simulator check when provider or database compatibility is itself at risk. ([Google, “Fixing a Test Hourglass”](https://testing.googleblog.com/2020/11/fixing-test-hourglass.html), lines 16–19, 93–99; [Software Engineering at Google, ch. 14](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch14.html), lines 25–37.)
- **Synthetic data:** One study’s abstract reports synthetic data did not reliably prevent inference attacks or could lose utility in evaluated models; a separate full-text article distinguishes expert-created test data from data synthesized using real records and argues for risk assessment. Neither supports a universal conclusion about all synthetic datasets. ([Stadler et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.07018), abstract; [Ricciato](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0282423X251412328), lines 37–66, 90–91.)

## 2. Weak points and suggested next step

- The collection does not define this app’s intended price/rounding policy, validity inclusivity, time-zone semantics, provider, scheduler, or critical journeys. The guide can identify test shapes and cases, but those expected results require product contracts.
- Time-control evidence is primarily framework/engine documentation. Temporal reports are unresolved or narrowly resolved user cases; they do not establish frequency or production impact. No source demonstrates that a fake-clock test alone verifies a production scheduler.
- Safe-data guidance is general. The accessible privacy materials do not provide a dataset-specific test-data recipe or certify a chosen dataset. The synthetic-data evidence is limited: one study was accessible only as an abstract, and the full-text analysis is not a systematic review. EDPS material and a synthetic-patient study could not be opened.
- Variable-output oracle research includes broad SEI guidance and specialized stochastic/parallel research software. It supports patterns such as invariants and metamorphic relations, but does not supply universal thresholds or prove which oracle suits a particular app. The full Barr et al. survey was unavailable; only the repository abstract was read. Practitioner issue reports are isolated cases, including older library versions.
- Collector C’s review escalated a lack of evidence for reminders/expiry over time and variable output.
