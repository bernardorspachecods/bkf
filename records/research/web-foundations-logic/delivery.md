# Business logic: pricing rules, states, approvals, versions and the audit trail

> **Read this first: rules that set prices and discounts produce documents customers accept, which carries financial and contractual consequences. This guide makes no legal conclusion about contract formation, signature level or the evidential value of an electronic acceptance, and a specialist must decide those points before anyone acts on them.** The legal evidence collected is primary text but not the current consolidated EU versions, and the judicial material is context-specific.

Objective served: a guide that supports Bernardo and the agents building the app when they decide how rules, states, approvals, versions and the audit trail are represented so they can change without rewriting the app. Mode: focused on the priorities. Who may approve belongs to Identity and access, the board as interface to Frontend structure, when reminders run to Background work, and billing and invoicing is out of scope.

Findings are marked as source, synthesis or inference. Citations give the source and section; the Source key at the end gives the links.

## 1. The answer

The evidence is documentation of rules, CPQ and quoting products (Salesforce CPQ, HubSpot, Odoo, RMS, SAP), state-machine libraries, PDF tooling, statutes and a few failure reports. It describes patterns and their limits; it gives no provider-neutral specification, so the guide lists the decisions and what each approach makes easy or hard to change.

### 1.1 Decisions to settle first

| Decision, in priority order | Main approaches supported by the collection | What becomes easier to change | What becomes harder or remains uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Represent prices and discounts | Versioned business data tables/records; decision tables or expression rules; calculation logic in application code | Business data can change without changing code; tables expose conditions and outcomes | Overlaps, order, date boundaries, recalculation and formulas need explicit semantics and validation; a table editor does not provide governance by itself |
| Represent quote states and review | State machine in application code; BPMN/workflow engine; product-specific quote lifecycle | Named transitions make allowed moves and exception review visible; a workflow engine can coordinate human and external work | State logic alone is not a durable history; engine deployment/migration adds operational work; approval authorization is out of scope |
| Preserve revisions, audit and sent documents | Mutable record plus snapshot/change history; separate quote revisions; append-only event stream; retained generated document | Snapshots support direct comparison and restoration; event streams support reconstruction; document records can retain prior outputs | There is no collected guarantee of immutable exact-file retention tied to every quote revision, nor of a quote’s exact price-rule version; full event sourcing adds complexity |
| Hand off accepted quotes | Record API/CRUD; event publishing; transactional outbox plus duplicate-safe consumer | APIs expose records; events notify consumers; outbox reduces the risk of losing a handoff when the local transaction commits | Delivery is generally at least once, so duplicates and reconciliation matter; the receiving system’s contract remains unknown |

The ordering and design implications above are **synthesis/inference**, not a sourced architecture mandate. The collection does support a focused boundary: treat tariffs and discount parameters as governed, versioned business data; keep calculation, precedence, date allocation, rounding, validation and fallback semantics in controlled code; record any quote-specific exception with its reason and review result. The data/code boundary synthesis appears in Collection B, “Evidence-based rule/data and code boundary”; its product examples are below.

### 1.2 Business-editable pricing data needs explicit calculation semantics

- **Source finding — hospitality-shaped table:** RMS separates rate values/calculation structure (Rate Table), business restrictions/packages (Rate Type), and applicability by property/category/date period (Rate Lookup). It supports derived rates, restrictions, additional guest fees, adult/child pricing and nights. A table is not bookable until linked through a lookup. This directly represents date periods, guest count and length of stay, while creating a configuration and precedence hierarchy. [RMS Help Centre, “Set up rate tables,” §§ Rate Table/Rate Type/Rate Lookup and configuration](https://support.rmscloud.com/hc/en-gb/articles/12101772292495-Set-up-rate-tables) (Collection B, §31, lines 633–640).
- **Source finding — configurable ERP/CRM records:** ERPNext pricing rules are records scoped by item/group, party, quantity/value, date, warehouse, company, currency and price list, with fixed prices, percentage/fixed discounts, margins or free products. Promotional Schemes handle tier slabs; overlaps have priority and apply-multiple/compounding settings; Python conditions extend the standard fields. This gives business-editable inputs, but also makes conflicts and custom executable conditions a governance boundary. [ERPNext, “Pricing Rule,” conditions, results, overlap and dynamic condition sections](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/pricing-rule) (Collection A, “Business-maintained price lists and discount rules,” lines 41–55).
- **Source finding — other configurable rules:** Salesforce CPQ models price rules as IF conditions and THEN actions against quote fields and related data; its example varies price by customer, geography, month and product. HubSpot quote rules cover product combinations, quantity, line-item volume, discounts and pricing boundaries, and can warn or block publication. [Salesforce Trailhead, “Get Started with Salesforce CPQ Price Rules,” “Pricing for Your Specific Business” and “Basic Price Rule Structure”](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/price-rules-in-salesforce-cpq/get-started-with-salesforce-cpq-price-rules) (Collection B, §3, lines 162–170); [HubSpot, “Configure and manage quote rules,” feature, DSL and warning/block sections](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/quotes/configure-quote-rules) (Collection A, lines 65–78).
- **Counterweight — line-specific discount maintenance:** A 2020 HubSpot community user asked how to discount selected quote lines while preserving add-ons, warranty and shipping, and raised the burden of maintaining duplicate products. The excerpt contains no answer, so this is an anecdotal user concern, not evidence of a current product limitation or a settled comparison of discount mechanisms. [HubSpot Community, “Discount by Line Item instead of Overall Price?,” user post](https://community.hubspot.com/t/discount-by-line-item-instead-of-overall-price/18584) (Collection A, “Business-maintained price lists and discount rules,” lines 77–80).
- **Source finding — table and expression representations:** DMN tables expose conditions and conclusions; Drools spreadsheet tables represent rules as rows and conditions/actions as columns, then compile into executable DRL. DMN hit policies make overlap behavior explicit: e.g. Unique rejects overlaps, First depends on row order, and Collect aggregates. JSONLogic stores expressions as JSON data, while CEL supplies deterministic, side-effect-free typed evaluation under host-provided bindings. These are different representations, not turnkey business authoring/governance systems. [OMG, DMN overview, lines 52–60](https://www.omg.org/dmn/); [Drools DMN, “Hit policies in DMN decision tables,” lines 341–362](https://docs.drools.org/latest/drools-docs/drools/DMN/index.html); [Drools language reference, “Spreadsheet decision tables,” lines 2757–2829](https://docs.drools.org/latest/drools-docs/drools/language-reference/index.html); [JSONLogic, “Data-Driven,” lines 49–86](https://jsonlogic.com/); [CEL language definition, deterministic evaluation/bindings, lines 3109–3191](https://github.com/cel-expr/cel-spec/blob/master/doc/langdef.md) (Collection A, “Decision tables and decision models,” lines 9–39; Collection B, §21, lines 542–549; Collection C, §6, lines 404–411).
- **Synthesis/inference — recommended change boundary:** Keep season periods, guest-count bands, stay-length bands, base prices, discount parameters, applicability and effective dates as versioned data. Keep the meaning of those fields and calculations in code: how a stay spanning seasons is priced, tie-breaking, inclusive boundaries, compounding, rounding, missing-rule behavior, and out-of-range checks. Store one-off discounts or overrides on the quote with attribution/reason and a review outcome rather than silently changing the tariff. The sources support the distinction, but do not prescribe this app’s schema or safe publication workflow (Collection B, “Evidence-based rule/data and code boundary,” lines 290–293).
- **Source finding — rule order is consequential:** Salesforce documents pricing calculation stages and a case where a rule evaluated too late fails to affect a dependent price; repeated manual Calculate clicks are called unacceptable. A Salesforce CPQ floor/ceiling example stores limits as records and blocks saving when a quote is outside them; it does not provide a human review path. [Salesforce Trailhead, “Optimizing CPQ Calculation Sequence,” calculation stages and example](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/price-rules-in-salesforce-cpq/sequence-price-rules-for-correct-calculations) (Collection C, §13, lines 467–474); [Salesforce Help, “Define Price Floor and Price Ceiling for Products in CPQ,” data object and validation rule](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=000381934&language=en_US&type=1) (Collection B, §22, lines 551–558).
- **Counterweight — changing a pricelist may not recalculate existing lines:** An Odoo practitioner account says changing a quote’s pricelist leaves current line prices unchanged, while newly added lines use the new pricelist. Treat this as a concrete recalculation hazard to check, not as current Odoo 18 behavior: the account describes older behavior, is not official documentation, and its full page could not be opened (the finding came from rendered search text). [Odoo forum, “The almost complete guide to pricelist behavior,” “Product Pricing Setup and Behavior” and “Behavior Details”](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/the-almost-complete-guide-to-pricelist-behavior-185877) (Collection B, §9, line 278).
- **Counterweight — environment-specific zero-price report:** In a 2017 Odoo forum thread, a user reported that a pricelist rule left a quotation price at zero in one Odoo Online environment although the same setup worked on a test server; a reply raised a possible currency mismatch and asked for more details. This is one historical troubleshooting report, not evidence of prevalence or current-version behavior. [Odoo forum, “Pricelist Rule not working,” user report and reply](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/pricelist-rule-not-working-117148) (Collection B, §9, lines 279–281).
- **Source finding — rules need tests and operational checks:** Drools DMN project compilation can detect table gaps/overlaps; static checks do not prove expected prices. Two issue reports describe performance problems for large DMN workloads after specific product versions/configurations, and a forum participant cautions that very large tables may be difficult to maintain. [Drools DMN documentation, static analysis, lines 956–964](https://docs.drools.org/latest/drools-docs/drools/DMN/index.html); [Camunda issue #62594, workload and evaluator issue](https://github.com/camunda/camunda/issues/62594); [Apache KIE issue #6870, regression report](https://github.com/apache/incubator-kie/issues/6870); [Camunda Forum, “Maximum number of Rules in a DMN,” lines 18–27](https://forum.camunda.io/t/maximum-number-of-rules-in-a-dmn/49962) (Collection B, §§10–12 and §21; Collection C, §§10–12).
- **Counterweight:** ERPNext issue reports describe surprising interactions between overlapping rules and “apply multiple”; Odoo issue #295858 reports date-valid rule precedence appearing dependent on creation order, but is newly opened and unverified. An Odoo forum scenario asks how one quote spanning high and low seasons should price each part of a stay, without supplying a solution. These are signals to encode and test precedence/date allocation, not evidence that current products universally fail. [ERPNext issues #49405 and #33210](https://github.com/frappe/erpnext/issues/49405), [Odoo issue #295858](https://github.com/odoo/odoo/issues/295858), [Odoo forum, “High Season & Low Season Price on Sales Order for overlapping dates”](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/high-season-low-season-price-on-sales-order-for-overlapping-dates-215450) (Collection A, lines 57–79; Collection B, §9, lines 274–287).

### 1.3 State and flow models make allowed moves visible; history is separate

- **Source finding — application state machines:** XState expresses event-triggered states and guarded branches; AASM and django-fsm store current state on a model and declare allowed transitions. AASM explicitly relies on a separate PaperTrail gem for state-change logging; django-fsm signals expose transition name/source/target but are not themselves a durable audit record. [XState v5 docs and Guards, machine examples and guard behavior](https://stately.ai/docs/guards); [AASM README, states/transitions and “Log State Changes”](https://github.com/aasm/aasm); [django-fsm-2 README, transitions/signals and optimistic locking](https://github.com/django-commons/django-fsm-2) (Collection A, “States, flows, and transition records,” lines 82–104).
- **Source finding — BPMN and workflow engine:** BPMN models tasks, gateways, events and participants; Camunda documents an exclusive gateway whose conditions are evaluated in model order, with a default path or an incident if none matches. User tasks can pause for human completion, and forms can bind to latest, process-deployed or tagged versions. Process/job workers can retry failed external work while a process remains at its current task. This makes cross-party flow and process position explicit but introduces engine and migration operations. [OMG BPMN overview and specification, process collaboration pp. 109–110, event gateway §13.3.4, business-rule task p. 414](https://www.omg.org/spec/BPMN/2.0/PDF/); [Camunda exclusive gateways, conditions/defaults](https://docs.camunda.io/docs/components/modeler/bpmn/exclusive-gateways/); [Camunda user tasks, form version binding](https://docs.camunda.io/docs/8.9/components/modeler/bpmn/user-tasks/); [Camunda processes, workers/retries](https://docs.camunda.io/docs/components/concepts/processes/) (Collection A, lines 96–104; Collection C, §§7–9, lines 413–448).
- **Source finding — product lifecycle:** HubSpot’s documented lifecycle includes Drafted, Published, Pending signature, Pending approval, Changes requested, Viewed, Pending countersignature and Accepted, plus activity events such as sent, viewed, signed, approval requested/rejected/approved, recalled and expired. It supports cloning and recall/edit in some states; accepted/signed quotes cannot be voided or deleted. Approval is a visible step, but availability is edition-specific and the product’s activity history is not shown to be tamper-evident. [HubSpot, “Manage quotes,” statuses, actions and activity sections](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/quotes/manage-quotes); [HubSpot, “Manage quote approvals,” review/change actions](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/quotes/manage-quote-approvals) (Collection B, §3, lines 176–186).
- **Synthesis/inference — minimal quote flow:** Keep an explicit current status and named transition event on the quote/revision. A practical flow supported by the examples is Draft → Calculated → Review required or Ready to send → Sent/awaiting customer → Accepted or Expired; review can return a quote for changes, and edits after sending produce a new revision. Record the reason and decision when a calculation is outside policy. This is a proposed synthesis; the collection does not determine exact status names, automatic expiry behavior or who is authorized to approve.
- **Source finding — engine versions are not quote versions:** Camunda process/decision deployments get new versions; running instances normally continue on their starting version unless migrated. Parallel versions preserve old execution context but increase operations complexity. This concerns workflow definitions, not tariff snapshots or quote revisions. [Camunda, “Versioning process definitions,” lines 61–90](https://docs.camunda.io/docs/components/best-practices/operations/versioning-process-definitions/) (Collection C, §15, lines 485–492).

### 1.4 Quote revisions, audit records and customer documents are distinct records

- **Source finding — product revision models:** Salesforce can hold multiple quote records under one opportunity, and Salesforce Insurance separately documents creating/comparing quote versions. SAP CPQ’s partially rendered documentation describes revisions as copies under a master quote, with later changes not propagating between copies; approval history is not copied. HubSpot supports clone/recall-edit workflows. Customer reports in HubSpot’s community describe cross-deal cloning as requiring repeated recreation of long quotes, and some users reported difficulty correcting quotes in signed or paid states, with duplication and resending reported as consequences. These are product-specific, historical reports rather than representative evidence; current HubSpot documentation is the stronger source for present state-dependent behavior. The examples show separate-copy or explicit-version approaches, but SAP access was partial and Salesforce Insurance behavior is product-specific. [Salesforce Help, “Manage Your Quotes,” lines 1132–1148](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=sf.quotes_manage_your_quotes.htm&language=en_US&type=5); [Salesforce Insurance, “Quote Versioning,” lines 3511–3529](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=ind.insurance_quote_versioning_612894.htm&language=en_US&type=5); [SAP CPQ, “Quote Revisions,” introductory paragraphs, partial rendered passage](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_CPQ/884885f05e6b4c8082254d4d9d63f19b/77e2d9d1fe01462682501343c89e092e.html); [HubSpot, “Manage quotes,” clone/recall sections](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/quotes/manage-quotes); [HubSpot Community, “Clone Quotes,” customer reports and product-manager update](https://community.hubspot.com/t/clone-quotes/14011); [HubSpot Community, “Recall and Edit Quotes,” customer reports](https://community.hubspot.com/t/recall-and-edit-quotes/59237) (Collection B, §3, lines 188–197; Collection B, §9, “Clone Quotes” and “Recall and Edit Quotes,” lines 284–286).
- **Source finding — validity belongs to a quote record in examples:** Salesforce quote records carry an expiration date; Odoo’s partially accessible documentation describes a default/template validity period that can be changed on a quote and displayed to the customer. Neither establishes version-specific expiry behavior when the quote changes. [Salesforce Trailhead, “Learn About Quotes,” lines 92–106](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/sf_cpq/sf_cpq_learn_quotes); [Odoo 18, “Quotation deadlines,” “Quotation expiration” and “Quotation template expiration,” search-rendered text only](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/sales/sales/sales_quotations/deadline.html) (Collection B, §3, lines 199–211).
- **Source finding — generated document retention and delivery:** Salesforce CPQ’s training says saving/emailing a generated PDF creates a Quote Document record with generation time and version number, and prior PDFs remain accessible. It does not promise retention for each quote edit, immutability, tamper evidence or legal sufficiency. HubSpot’s quote share flow does not attach a generated PDF over 20 MB to the email, though the buyer can access the quote by link; this is a delivery caveat, not evidence that the link retains the exact sent PDF bytes. WeasyPrint and Puppeteer document rendering; they do not provide storage, retention or sent-file linkage. [Salesforce Trailhead, “Get Started with Quote Templates in Salesforce CPQ,” document records and prior versions](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/quote-templates-in-salesforce-cpq/get-started-with-quote-templates-in-salesforce-cpq); [HubSpot, “Create and send quotes,” finalization and sharing](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/quotes/create-and-send-quotes?ctl=Login); [WeasyPrint, “Generate Specialized PDFs” and metadata](https://doc.courtbouillon.org/weasyprint/stable/common_use_cases.html?highlight=security); [Puppeteer, `Page.pdf()`](https://pptr.dev/api/puppeteer.page.pdf) (Collection B, §25, lines 578–585; §3, lines 203–211; §6, lines 232–244).
- **Source finding — change history models:** Audit-log rows can record change/event metadata; Fowler notes that growing logs become difficult to query/reconstruct. PaperTrail and django-simple-history store selected model versions/snapshots and can expose actor, timestamp and diffs when configured. Camunda’s audit log records actor, operation, entity and time, but variable values may be omitted and unchanged variables excluded. Salesforce Field History Tracking is selective, starts only when enabled, has value/field limits, and may not capture initial manually entered values as changes. [Fowler, “Audit Log,” “How it Works” and “When to Use It”](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/AuditLog.html); [PaperTrail README, usage/API](https://github.com/paper-trail-gem/paper_trail); [django-simple-history, user tracking and history diffing](https://django-simple-history.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_tracking.html); [Camunda, “Recorded operations” and “Operation data structure”](https://docs.camunda.io/docs/components/audit-log/overview/recorded-operations/); [Salesforce, “Field History Tracking Overview” and creation behavior](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=xcloud.tracking_field_history.htm&language=en_US&type=5) (Collection B, §4, lines 213–230; Collection C, §§16–17, lines 494–510; Collection B, §27, lines 596–603).
- **Source finding — event sourcing alternative:** An append-only stream can reconstruct state and feed external consumers, but adds schema evolution, replay, read-model and testing costs; at-least-once consumers need idempotency. Microsoft guidance advises against adopting it for straightforward CRUD solely to get an audit log. [Microsoft Azure Architecture Center, “Event Sourcing Pattern,” definition, benefits/complexities and unsuitable cases](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/event-sourcing) (Collection B, §28, lines 605–612).
- **Synthesis/inference — revision chain:** On each material change, create a new quote revision with its own calculated values, validity date and rule-set identifier; retain prior revisions. Render the customer document from that revision and persist the exact output bytes and send/acceptance events with it. A document hash and template/render metadata would help identify the retained artifact. Salesforce CPQ document records support part of this pattern, while the complete chain is an inference and **no collected source guarantees immutable exact bytes sent against every revision or links a revision to the precise tariff/rule version** (Collection B, “Quote revision and exact sent-document model,” lines 294–297; Collection B, §25).

### 1.5 Acceptance evidence and downstream handoff have separate boundaries

- **Source finding — public law:** Portugal’s Decreto-Lei 12/2021 distinguishes electronic documents, qualified electronic signatures and other electronic evidence; a qualified signature has handwritten-signature equivalence and statutory presumptions, while the probative value of other documents falls under general legal rules. The original Official Journal eIDAS text says electronic signatures/documents cannot be denied effect solely because they are electronic; the e-Commerce Directive covers electronic contracting and availability of terms for storage/reproduction. These primary sources do not decide whether this specific quote, acceptance method or retention practice is sufficient. [Decreto-Lei n.º 12/2021, Arts. 2–3, pp. 5–6](https://files.diariodarepublica.pt/1s/2021/02/02700/0000400016.pdf); [eIDAS Regulation 910/2014, Arts. 2(3), 25 and 46, original Official Journal text](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2014/910/pdfs/eur_20140910_adopted_en.pdf); [Directive 2000/31/EC, Arts. 9–11, Official Journal pp. 16–17](https://uoou.gov.cz/media/directive-2000-31-ec.pdf) (Collection C, §§1–3, lines 357–382).
- **Source finding — courts apply context and evidence to the facts:** The Portuguese Supreme Court treated conduct in a construction estimate dispute as tacit acceptance; in a services dispute, Coimbra found silence did not accept proposed prices absent an applicable basis, while another Lisbon decision treated specific acceptance emails and later payment confirmation as acceptance of a particular proposal. A separate Lisbon appellate dispute concerned whether a signed acceptance form incorporated a proposal as the final contract or only an interim arrangement pending a fuller contract, and whether the signer had authority to bind the party; the decision records these as disputed issues and is not a settled general holding. A 2026 Guimarães loan decision involved a validated qualified signature but still assessed proof in that case. CJEU material concerns a click-wrapped jurisdiction clause where terms could be saved/printed and qualified-signature evidentiary treatment; it does not establish a general quote rule. [STJ, construction estimate, reasoning on offer/tacit acceptance, lines 72–83](https://www.dgsi.pt/jstj.nsf/954f0ce6ad9dd8b980256b5f003fa814/36d6895c17f9f8a28025793c0044572b); [Coimbra appeal, email prices and silence, lines 107–115](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrc.nsf/8fe0e606d8f56b22802576c0005637dc/c8a83c201498b9bb80258c530059ee57); [Lisbon appeal, acceptance form and whether it formed a final contract, lines 50–59 and reasoning from line 62](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrl.nsf/-/7DBD71366C1AFE0F802581DE0050A0FF); [Lisbon appeal, proposal acceptance by email/performance, lines 251–261](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrl.nsf/e6e1f17fa82712ff80257583004e3ddc/6bb34aaa00ec328d80258c8b003a0e3e); [Guimarães appeal, signature validation and legal framework, lines 126–164](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrg.nsf/86c25a698e4e7cb7802579ec004d3832/6cf8ee4155edca1980258e2100380d8f); [CJEU, El Majdoub thematic fiche, PDF p. 16](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2019-06/fiche_thematique_-commerce_electronique_et_obligations_contractuelles_-_en.pdf); [CJEU, V.B. Trade, C-466/22, Article 25 interpretation](https://infocuria.curia.europa.eu/tabs/redirect/juris/documents.jsf?num=C-466%2F22) (Collection B, §12, lines 298–327).
- **Legal divergence/limit:** Statutes establish the non-discrimination principle and distinguish signature levels and evidentiary weight; they do not establish a universal rule that click acceptance suffices, which signature level a particular quote needs, or a retention duration. DocuSign and Adobe broadly summarize the statutory distinction but are providers, not legal authorities, and contain legal-advice disclaimers. EUR-Lex consolidated versions could not be opened; the eIDAS source is the original 2014 text. Several independent primary legal texts were consulted, but there is no judicial authority directly settling this app’s use case. **A Portuguese legal specialist must decide the applicable terms, form, acceptance evidence and retention.** (Collection C, §§1–5 and stop note, lines 357–400; Collection C, “Stop note,” lines 419–421).
- **Source finding — record APIs and event APIs:** Frappe REST exposes generic DocType CRUD and standard metadata such as owner, created/modified timestamps, modified_by and status; Salesforce sObject REST is record-oriented. Salesforce Pub/Sub/platform events offer event delivery, but high-volume events are asynchronous and at least once, with 72-hour retention and ordering only within a publish request. An event notification is not a durable quote archive. [Frappe REST API, CRUD and record metadata](https://docs.frappe.io/framework/user/en/api/rest); [Salesforce REST API, sObject Rows](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/api-rest/guide/resources-sobject-retrieve.html); [Salesforce Pub/Sub API](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/pub-sub-api/guide/intro.html); [Salesforce Platform Events, delivery, retention and ordering](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/platform-events/guide/platform-events-intro-emp.html) (Collection A, “External system handoff and API boundaries,” lines 108–130).
- **Source finding — durable retry pattern:** AWS’s transactional outbox writes the domain update and outbound payload in one database transaction, then publishes it. A crash after publishing but before outbox cleanup can duplicate delivery; consumers need idempotency and ordering decisions. Stripe’s idempotency keys return the first saved response for repeated requests after endpoint execution begins, subject to product-specific key retention and parameter rules. [AWS Prescriptive Guidance, “Transactional outbox pattern,” architecture and delivery sections](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/cloud-design-patterns/transactional-outbox.html); [Stripe API, “Idempotent requests,”](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests) (Collection B, §29, lines 614–621).
- **Synthesis/inference — handoff boundary:** Persist acceptance and the outbound event/payload atomically, give the event a durable identifier, retry delivery, and require the receiver to handle duplicates or provide an idempotency contract. Keep the accepted quote revision and customer document in durable app storage; use APIs/events for transfer and notification, not as the only archive. Receiver behavior and reconciliation remain integration-specific.

### 1.6 Scope pointer

Identity and access owns approver authorization; Frontend Structure owns the board/interface; Background Work owns reminder timing; billing and invoicing are outside this brief.

### 1.7 Where sources diverge

- **Editable rule data versus rule logic in code:** DMN/spreadsheet/record-based models expose decisions for editing; CEL/JSONLogic can serialize expressions; Salesforce supports a custom JavaScript calculator and ERPNext allows Python conditions. The sources do not agree on a universal “business user can edit” boundary. The stable distinction is that commercial inputs can be data while calculation semantics remain controlled code (synthesis above). Fowler warns that chaining rules can make behavior hard to reason about; the observed rule-precedence issues make the counterweight concrete, though product- and version-specific (Collection A, lines 30–39 and 57–79; Collection B, §21 and §22).
- **Workflow diagram/engine versus application state machine:** BPMN/Camunda models participants, tasks and retries across a process; AASM/django-fsm/XState keep transition logic close to application models. A model or engine can define current paths, but the sources consistently separate that from durable quote history. The collection has no evidence that this app needs a standalone workflow engine (Collection A, lines 82–104; Collection C, §§7–9).
- **Change log versus snapshots versus event stream:** Logs efficiently record selected events but can become difficult to reconstruct; snapshot libraries offer prior state/diffs; event sourcing offers replay and consumer feeds at higher operational cost. Platform audit trails are selective, and none of these automatically preserves the exact customer file. This is a real modeling choice, not interchangeable terminology (Collection B, §§4 and 27–28; Collection C, §§16–17).
- **Electronic acceptance versus sufficiency of proof:** Primary law recognizes electronic records and differentiates qualified from other signatures, while provider explainers discuss practical transaction evidence. None resolves this particular offer’s legal requirements. Keep this as a legal decision, not a conclusion inferred from a product’s “accepted” status (Collection C, §§1–5).

## 2. Weak points and suggested next step

- Exact immutable retention of the bytes sent, linked to an immutable quote revision and its validity date, remains unresolved. Salesforce CPQ shows accessible prior document versions, but no source guarantees this entire chain for every revision. The design recommendation to save the exact PDF is an inference; verify implementation and retention behavior directly.
- No source establishes an immutable link from each quote revision to the exact price-rule/tariff version used. This is essential if later changes to seasonal prices or discount rules must not rewrite the explanation of an accepted offer.
