# Plan work

- A plan states the intended outcome, the boundaries, the work needed, and how completion will be recognized, in terms someone else can check and written before the work starts, so reviewers can test against them without relying on the producer's account. Keep it as short as the work allows; add phases, research, review, or deliverables only for a concrete need, and do not create a parallel tracker when a canonical plan already owns the work.
- Do not choose the plan's format for the user: ask whether they want a conversation plan or a durable plan, explaining the difference neutrally. Likewise recommend workflow settings (how many subplans, whether research is on, how often to check in) with their effect, let the user choose, and ask before adding a local exception.
- Present the finished plan with its execution contract and wait for the user's agreement on scope, outputs, sequence, gates, and settings before any assigned work starts. That agreement authorizes in-scope assignments but does not waive a required checkpoint; pause earlier for a blocker, an unmet requirement, or a decision that changes approved scope, and if a request conflicts with the plan's settings, stop and reconcile the plan first.
- Agreed requirements are mandatory. Resolve minor issues within the approved scope. Blocked means paused for a user decision; it is not a final state and not a way to accept incomplete work. If a requirement cannot be met within scope, stop and name the requirement and the blocker.
- Overall plan: it maps the whole effort: the objective, the approach and boundaries, the current phase (the pending decision or handoff and the next action; when blocked, what blocks it and what will unblock it, not a restatement that work is paused), a concise ordered phase map linking to the subplans, and the global completion gate. A phase has no brief of its own; its review uses the criteria defined in the [plan workflow](/workflow-types/plan.md#stage-notes). Do not repeat subplan briefs, or the links in a second tree or index.
- Subplan: the executable brief for one bounded task: objective, scope, relevant inputs (referred to by ID), expected output, and completion condition. Do not restate the overall objective. Record completion, verification, and material differences from the brief in a short outcome section without repeating the objective; create a separate result document only when a standalone deliverable must be consulted on its own.
