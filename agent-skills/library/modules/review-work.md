# Review work

Check a prior output against criteria and report findings.

- Form your own view before you read the work. From the brief and criteria
  alone, write your own criteria or tests, or outline how you would solve it,
  then compare. Reading the work first anchors you to it and makes you more
  likely to confirm it.
- A different agent is not an independent check by itself. Independence comes
  from what you check against, such as criteria, tests, or an outside source,
  and not from being a different reviewer. Prefer evidence the producer did
  not control.
- Independent reviewers of the same target do not read one another's
  conclusions before submitting their own.
- Ground findings in checkable evidence, such as a file and location, a
  quoted passage, an observed behavior, or a reproducible result, and explain
  how the evidence supports the finding.
- Do not edit what you review. Report findings to the owner.
- State what your check covers and what it does not, and what risk remains.
  A check that found nothing is not proof that nothing is wrong.
- Disagreement between your view and the work is a signal, not a verdict.
  Say which parts differ and why, and leave the decision to the owner, a
  test, or a human.
- Follow the assignment for scope, criteria, context, and output, and ask the coordinator to resolve any gap before reviewing. Confirm which targets are in scope and which materials are only context, and keep the targets stable during the review.
- Assess the work against the assigned criteria only: do not assess an individual agent's performance, silently expand the scope, or add criteria as if they were assigned. Report a relevant concern outside the criteria separately.
- For each finding, say whether it calls for a correction, is optional advice, or is informational. List any criterion that could not be assessed, and use severity labels only when they help the coordinator act.
- After rework, recheck the affected items, then run a quick regression check that the rework did not change what it should not.
- Label the strength of each finding: a breach of a documented rule is a hard violation, while a general baseline heuristic is a judgement call. A documented local rule overrides a general baseline, and anything tooling already enforces is skipped.
