# Conclude from evidence

- Support status of a claim: supported (directly, adequate quality, corroboration in proportion to importance, no unresolved material conflict; a high-importance claim needs more than one cluster), partly supported (narrower scope or one cluster for a high-importance claim), contested (credible evidence conflicts and differences of method, scope, or date do not resolve it), or insufficient evidence (say what was searched; this is not proof of absence).
- Confidence (high, moderate, low) is how far the evidence supports the conclusion as stated, judged on directness, source quality, independence, fit to scope, and unresolved conflict. It is not citation count, prestige, wording, or length. Use the labels in an answer only where the meaning is clear; otherwise explain the uncertainty in plain words.
- Write a finding per claim that answers it, not a sequence of source summaries. Where sources conflict, check whether date, scope, method, population, or definitions explain it. Label what is source-reported, what is a cross-source synthesis, and what is your own inference; for a recommendation, name the evidence and the judgments it adds.
- Audit a conclusion against its evidence, not the author's intent: reopen the source and confirm the passage is verbatim and that the claim as written in the answer is what it supports (wording, method, population, scope, period); check dates, corrections, and retractions; check that each citation points to the entry supporting that exact sentence; re-trace independence; for high-importance claims run a targeted search for conflicting evidence, null results, and undisclosed conflicts of interest, recording each query and its result, including no result; check that no page's framing shaped a conclusion; and check that the stated status and confidence do not exceed the evidence.
