# Align intent

Inside a plan, a delegated task or a review, stop reading: the brief already carries the intent.

## Intent and alignment

- Before work that is more than a small change, settle the intent with the user, so that afterwards you can work without confirming small things. A direction ("improve the docs") is not an intent: propose the nearest step you can close.
- The intent states: what is wanted and why, meaning the use the result serves; what exists when it is done and when you stop; what is out of scope; what is already decided and not reopened; and what would make you stop and ask.
- Ask until a wrong guess would send the whole thread the wrong way, then assume and continue. Ask the one blocking question, not several.
- Restate the intent in your own words and have the user confirm it before you start. Vague intent cannot be judged afterwards.
- Long or delegated work (a plan, research, a council, a review, anything handed to other agents) keeps the intent in one goal file that every agent reads and the user confirms. Small work restates it in the conversation and needs no file.
- Once confirmed, work inside it without status messages or small questions. Stop only for a decision that changes the intent or the scope, or for a requirement that cannot be met within them, and say it short: the decision, your recommendation, what it costs.
- Intent can change, with the reason on record. Revising is legitimate; drifting without saying is not. Stop midway only when the intent stops holding.
